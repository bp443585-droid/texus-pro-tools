The Lexical Parsing Rule: Natural language number expressions operate through hierarchical multiplication and addition. Words like thousand, million, and billion act as scale multipliers that partition the phrase into manageable accumulator blocks before final summation.

The Computational Core: Lexical Tokenization and Accumulator Math

When a user inputs a natural language number string, our high-performance client-side script executes a rigorous multi-stage transformation pipeline:

1. Normalization and Sanitization

The raw string is converted to lowercase, and punctuation marks (such as commas, periods, or non-breaking spaces) are cleaned. Hyphens connecting compound tens and units (e.g., "twenty-five") are normalized into standard spaces or token delimiters to ensure uniform array processing.

2. Dictionary Mapping and Tokenization

The input is split into individual word tokens. Each word is checked against a comprehensive hash dictionary of base numerals (`one` to `nine`), teens (`eleven` to `nineteen`), tens (`twenty` to `ninety`), and scale magnitudes (`hundred`, `thousand`, `million`, `billion`, `trillion`). Conjunction words like `and` are safely ignored by the parser.

3. Scale Multiplier Accumulation Loop

As the parser iterates through the token array, it maintains running current and total accumulators. When it encounters scale words like thousand, it multiplies the current subtotal by 1,000 and adds it to the grand total, allowing complex expressions like "two million three hundred thousand" to resolve without math errors.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Core Diagnostic Segment

How does the converter handle conjunctions like "and" in number words?

In British and American English, the word "and" is frequently inserted before tens and units (e.g., "one hundred and twenty"). Our lexical parser automatically recognizes and skips conjunction tokens without interrupting the mathematical summation loop.
