The Technical Core: Yaw Scale Coefficients and Math Formulas

To convert sensitivity between games accurately, we must understand how game engines translate raw mouse counts into rotational degrees:

1. Understanding DPI and Counts Per Inch

Dots Per Inch (DPI) or Counts Per Inch (CPI) defines how many hardware sensor reports your mouse sends to your operating system per linear inch of physical movement. Changing your DPI directly impacts your overall pointer velocity unless compensated by your in-game multiplier.

2. Engine Yaw Constants

Every game engine multiplies mouse counts by a specific engine yaw coefficient to determine camera rotation per count. For instance, CS2 utilizes a standard yaw of `0.022` degrees per count at a default 1-to-1 ratio, while Valorant applies a scalar factor calibrated to `0.07` at standard resolutions.

3. The Conversion Formula

The transposer calculates the physical 360 distance in centimeters using the formula:

Distance (cm) = (360 * 2.54) / (DPI * InGameSensitivity * YawConstant)

It then solves this equation backwards for your target game to output the exact sensitivity required.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Optimization Diagnostic Segment

Why does my converted sensitivity feel slightly different despite matching 360 distances?

Minor perceptual differences can arise from contrasting Field of View (FOV) settings, aspect ratios, and vertical versus horizontal monitor scaling. Ensure your target game's FOV matches your source game's vertical or horizontal radians for a seamless transition.

Should I change my mouse DPI when transposing sensitivity?

It is generally recommended to keep your mouse DPI locked to your preferred sensor sweet spot (commonly 800 or 1600 DPI where hardware interpolation is minimal) and adjust only the in-game sensitivity slider.
