Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Diagnostic Segment

Why is the NATO phonetic alphabet used instead of traditional names (like 'A as in Apple')?

Ad-hoc spelling alphabets ('A for apple', 'B for boy') cause confusion because different speakers use different words, and regional accents can distort everyday nouns. The NATO/ICAO alphabet was scientifically tested across diverse linguistic groups to ensure maximum distinctiveness even over heavily degraded radio frequencies.

How are numbers pronounced in aviation and emergency radio procedures?

In official radiotelephony, numbers follow specific pronunciation rules to avoid confusion over static: 0 is pronounced "Zero", 1 is "Wun", 2 is "Too", 3 is "Tree", 4 is "Fower", 5 is "Fife", 6 is "Six", 7 is "Seven", 8 is "Ait", and 9 is "Niner".
