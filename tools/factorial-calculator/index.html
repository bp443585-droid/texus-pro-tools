The Mathematics of Factorials: Permutations, Combinations, and Combinatorial Growth Laws

The factorial of a non-negative integer $n$, denoted by $n!$, is the product of all positive integers less than or equal to $n$. As one of the most fundamental operations in combinatorics, probability theory, algebra, and computer science algorithm analysis, factorials dictate how items can be arranged, selected, or ordered. Understanding the exponential growth curves of factorial functions is essential for designing efficient software algorithms, solving probability models, and analyzing recursive computing complexities.

This extensive reference manual explores the formal mathematical definitions of factorials, examines Stirling's approximation for extremely large values, outlines permutations and combinations formulas, and highlights the mechanics of our high-precision client-side computation engine.

The Zero Factorial Axiom: By convention of the empty product in mathematics, the factorial of zero ($0!$) evaluates strictly to 1, ensuring consistency across binomial coefficient expansions and exponential generating functions.

Why does $0!$ equal 1 instead of 0?

In mathematics, $0!$ is defined as an empty product. When multiplying no numbers together, the multiplicative identity is 1. Furthermore, formulas for permutations ($P(n,r) = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$) would fail if $0!$ evaluated to zero.
