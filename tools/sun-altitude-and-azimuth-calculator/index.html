The Observer Horizon Framework: Solar altitude measures the angular height of the sun above the geometric horizon (0° at horizon, 90° directly overhead), while solar azimuth measures the compass bearing of the sun along the horizon (0° North, 90° East, 180° South, 270° West).

The Technical Core: Astronomical Equations and Solar Angles

Computing the exact position of the sun requires tracking Earth's axial tilt (approximately 23.44°) and its elliptical orbit around the sun throughout the 365.25-day year. The primary variables computed by our precision algorithm include:

1. Day of Year and Fractional Time

The calculation begins by determining the exact fractional day of the year ($n$) from the user's input date and Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). This accounts for leap years and orbital progression.

2. Solar Declination ($\delta$)

Solar declination is the angle between the equator of the earth and the line connecting the centers of the earth and the sun. It varies smoothly between -23.44° during the winter solstice and +23.44° during the summer solstice, calculated via Fourier approximations of Earth's orbital eccentricity.

3. Equation of Time (EoT)

Because Earth's orbit is elliptical and its rotational axis is tilted, apparent solar time differs from clock time by up to 16 minutes throughout the year. The Equation of Time corrects for this temporal discrepancy.

4. Altitude and Azimuth Transformation

Using the observer's latitude ($\phi$), local solar time converted into hour angle ($H$), and solar declination ($\delta$), standard spherical coordinate transformations yield the altitude ($h$) and azimuth ($A$):

$$\sin(h) = \sin(\phi)\sin(\delta) + \cos(\phi)\cos(\delta)\cos(H)$$

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Solar Calculation Segment

Why is Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) used for solar calculations?

UTC provides a universal temporal baseline free from local political time zone boundaries, daylight saving time shifts, and irregular provincial borders. By converting local dates to UTC and incorporating longitude offsets, the solar geometry engine computes exact hour angles anywhere on Earth without ambiguity.

How does solar azimuth determine photovoltaic panel orientation?

In the Northern Hemisphere, solar panels generally yield maximum annual energy when oriented due South (azimuth 180°). In the Southern Hemisphere, panels face due North (azimuth 0° or 360°). Knowing the exact sun azimuth throughout the day also helps identify morning or evening shadow obstructions from nearby buildings or terrain.

Does this calculator account for atmospheric refraction?

Atmospheric refraction bends light rays passing through denser air layers near the horizon, making the sun appear slightly higher than its true geometric position (by up to 0.5° when near the horizon). High-precision ephemeris tools apply standard atmospheric corrections when altitude is close to zero.
