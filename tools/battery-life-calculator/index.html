The Physics of Energy Storage: Decoding Battery Capacity, Current Drain, Peukert's Law, and Runtime Estimations

Designing embedded electronics, sizing backup power supply units, or calculating smartphone endurance requires a thorough engineering understanding of electrochemical storage metrics. Battery capacity ratings—expressed in milliamp-hours (mAh) or watt-hours (Wh)—represent total potential charge reserves. However, calculating true operational runtime involves much more than a simple division equation. Internal resistance, Peukert's law discharge curves, temperature fluctuations, and inverter conversion losses all dictate how long a power source will actually last under load.

This extensive guide explores the fundamental mathematics behind battery longevity, reviews performance behaviors across different chemical compositions (Lithium-Ion, Lead-Acid, NiMH), analyzes energy efficiency losses, and outlines how to optimize power delivery in modern hardware systems.

The Fundamental Runtime Equation: Base operational hours are calculated by dividing the usable energy capacity by the active current draw, adjusted by an operational safety multiplier to account for real-world environmental losses.

The Technical Core: Electrical Formulas and Conversion Calculations

When power data passes through our client-side calculation pipeline, the script normalizes all metrics into standard energy units to ensure precise mathematical outputs:

1. Milliamp-Hour to Watt-Hour Normalization

Because devices often consume power measured in watts while batteries report capacity in milliamp-hours, our engine bridges the gap using nominal voltage: $\text{Wh} = \frac{\text{mAh} \times \text{Voltage}}{1000}$. This unifies energy potential into a direct work metric.

2. Current Draw vs Power Dissipation Mapping

If a load is entered in watts (W), the script converts it to current draw using Ohm's Law ($I = \frac{P}{V}$), ensuring compatibility whether your device parameters are specified in amperes or watts.

3. Operational Efficiency Factors

No power system is 100% efficient. Voltage drop under heavy loads, circuit heat dissipation, and DC-DC regulator conversion overhead all deplete usable energy. Applying an efficiency factor (typically 80% to 90% for Lithium systems and 50% to 70% for Lead-Acid) prevents unexpected power failures.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Core Diagnostic Segment

Why does my battery drain faster when my device is under heavy load?

High discharge rates increase internal resistance within the battery cells, leading to greater voltage sag and thermal energy loss. According to Peukert's Law, drawing current faster than the rated 1C or 0.2C rate reduces the total effective amp-hour capacity of the battery.

How do I convert milliamp-hours (mAh) to watt-hours (Wh)?

Multiply the capacity in mAh by the nominal voltage of the battery, then divide by 1,000. For example, a 10,000 mAh power bank operating at 3.7 volts stores $10,000 \times 3.7 / 1000 = 37 \text{ Wh}$ of electrical energy.
