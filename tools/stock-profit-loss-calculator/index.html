The Technical Core: Formulas and Return Computation Metrics

When financial data passes through our client-side calculation pipeline, the script executes robust mathematical formulas instantly:

1. Gross Investment Valuation

The initial capital deployed (Total Investment) equals the purchase price per share multiplied by the total number of shares acquired. Similarly, the gross exit value equals the selling price multiplied by the share quantity.

2. Fee Deduction & Brokerage Adjustments

Modern brokerages charge commissions upon entry and exit. Net profit is calculated by taking the gross sale proceeds, subtracting the total purchase cost, and further deducting all accumulated transaction fees.

3. Return on Investment (ROI) Percentages

The percentage gain or loss represents the net financial return relative to the initial capital deployed, expressed as a standard financial ratio.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Core Diagnostic Segment

How do brokerage commissions impact short-term day trading returns?

In high-frequency day trading, brokerage commissions represent a significant percentage of total capital. Because short-term price fluctuations are often modest, high per-trade fees can quickly turn winning trades into net losses. Calculating net returns before entering orders is essential for profitability.

What is the difference between ROI and annualized return?

Return on Investment (ROI) measures total percentage gain across the entire holding period, regardless of duration. Annualized return scales that gain to a yearly rate, allowing investors to fairly compare a 3-month trade against a 5-year investment.
