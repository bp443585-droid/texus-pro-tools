The Golden Rule of eDPI: Effective DPI (eDPI) is the product of your hardware mouse DPI multiplied by your software in-game sensitivity. As long as your eDPI remains constant, the physical distance required to perform a 360-degree rotation on your mousepad remains identical.

The Technical Core: Sensor Resolution, Hardware Tracking, and Math Formulas

To convert sensitivity accurately when modifying your mouse DPI, you must understand how software and hardware scales intersect across modern game engines:

1. Hardware DPI vs Software Sensitivity

DPI (Dots Per Inch) measures how many physical pixels your mouse sensor registers per linear inch of physical movement across a mousepad. In-game sensitivity acts as a multiplier applied to raw count data before it moves the camera vector in your 3D viewport. Doubling your mouse DPI while cutting your in-game sensitivity in half yields the exact same camera rotation velocity.

2. The eDPI Calculation Equation

The formula for calculating effective dots per inch is straightforward: `eDPI = Mouse DPI × In-Game Sensitivity`. For example, if you run 800 DPI with 0.5 sensitivity in Valorant, your eDPI equals 400. If you upgrade your mouse and switch to 1600 DPI, dividing your target eDPI (400) by your new hardware DPI (1600) gives you your new required sensitivity: 0.25.

3. Game Engine Multipliers

Different game developers code their sensitivity scales differently. For instance, Source engine titles (CS:GO, CS2, Team Fortress 2) and Valorant share a direct linear scale. However, games like Overwatch or Apex Legends utilize distinct sensitivity scaling coefficients, meaning a sensitivity of 2.0 in one engine produces wildly different rotation speeds in another.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Core Diagnostic Segment

Is a higher mouse DPI always better for gaming performance?

Not necessarily. While ultra-high DPI settings (like 16,000 or 25,600 DPI) reduce sensor latency by minuscule fractions of a millisecond, they can amplify hand tremors and pick up micro-vibrations from your desk. Most professional esports players stick to 400, 800, or 1600 DPI as the optimal sweet spot for tracking fidelity and sensor smoothing stability.

Does changing my mouse polling rate affect my sensitivity?

No, changing your polling rate (from 125Hz up to 1000Hz, 4000Hz, or 8000Hz) alters how many times per second your mouse reports its position to your computer, but it does not change the physical distance required for a 360-degree turn. However, higher polling rates consume more CPU resources.
