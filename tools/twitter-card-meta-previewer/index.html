The Technical Core: Anatomy of Twitter Meta Tags

Twitter utilizes the `twitter:` namespace alongside standard Open Graph (`og:`) properties to determine how links display in timelines. When configuring your document head, ensuring correct syntax prevents crawler errors:

1. `twitter:card` (Card Type Definition)

This mandatory attribute tells the X crawler whether to render a compact square thumbnail (`summary`) or an immersive widescreen hero banner (`summary_large_image`).

2. `twitter:title` and `twitter:description`

While crawlers often fall back to Open Graph tags (`og:title`), explicitly setting Twitter-specific strings allows you to craft tighter, action-driven copy tailored specifically to X's fast-moving audience.

3. `twitter:image` and Domain Attribution

High-resolution image URLs ensure crisp rendering across desktop and mobile app viewports. Including creator handles (`twitter:creator`) and site attribution (`twitter:site`) ties shared content directly back to your brand profile.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Core Diagnostic Segment

Why is my Twitter card showing up as a plain link instead of a rich image preview?

Plain text fallback usually occurs due to missing `twitter:card` tags, incorrect absolute image URLs (must start with `https://`), server blocks preventing Twitter's user-agent bot (`Twitterbot`) from scraping your page, or restrictive robots.txt rules.

What are the exact recommended image dimensions for Twitter cards?

For Summary Cards with Large Image, use an aspect ratio of 1.91:1 with a minimum resolution of 600x314 pixels (1200x628 pixels is ideal for retina displays). For standard square Summary Cards, use a 1:1 aspect ratio with a minimum of 144x144 pixels.
