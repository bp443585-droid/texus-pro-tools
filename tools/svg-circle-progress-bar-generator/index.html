The Mathematical Core: SVG Stroke Dasharray and Offset Mechanics

Understanding how browsers calculate circle progress requires mastering two key SVG path attributes: `stroke-dasharray` and `stroke-dashoffset`.

1. Calculating Path Circumference

When you draw a circle with a specific radius `r` inside an SVG tag, its total perimeter length is calculated as `2 * Math.PI * r`. For instance, a circle with a radius of 45 pixels yields a total circumference of approximately `282.74` pixels.

2. Applying Stroke Dasharray

The `stroke-dasharray` property turns a solid stroke into a dashed line by specifying alternating lengths of dashes and gaps. When we set both the dash length and the gap length equal to the circle's total circumference (`282.74`), the stroke appears as one unbroken solid ring.

3. Manipulating Dash Offset for Progress

The `stroke-dashoffset` property shifts where the dash pattern begins along the path. By starting with an offset equal to the full circumference (hiding the entire progress stroke) and progressively reducing the offset toward zero, the browser animates the stroke filling up smoothly based on percentage values.
