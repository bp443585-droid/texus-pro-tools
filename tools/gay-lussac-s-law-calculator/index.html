The Thermodynamics of Gay-Lussac's Law: Pressure-Temperature Proportionality in Isochoric Gas Systems

Understanding how gases behave under constant volume constraints is fundamental to chemical engineering, mechanical thermodynamics, and atmospheric science. Formulated by French chemist and physicist Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac in 1802, Gay-Lussac's Law (sometimes referred to as Amontons's Law) establishes that the absolute pressure of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature, provided that the volume remains entirely constant.

This comprehensive technical reference explores the mathematical foundations of isochoric transformations, examines kinetic molecular interpretations of pressure increase, reviews temperature scale requirements, and outlines clean methodologies for solving complex state-change problems in industrial and academic laboratories.

The Isochoric Proportionality Factor: When a rigid container is heated, gas molecules gain kinetic energy, colliding with container walls more frequently and with greater momentum. This directly elevates internal pressure without altering spatial dimensions ($P \propto T$).

Mathematical Formulation and Fundamental Constants

Mathematically, Gay-Lussac's Law is expressed as the constant ratio between pressure ($P$) and absolute temperature ($T$):

$$\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$$
