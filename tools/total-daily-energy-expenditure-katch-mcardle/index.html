The Technical Core: Mathematical Equations and Metabolic Components

Calculating your total daily energy expenditure involves two distinct phases: determining your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and scaling that baseline value by your daily physical activity coefficient.

1. Lean Body Mass (LBM) Computation

The calculator first determines your lean mass by subtracting your body fat weight from your total body weight.
`\text{LBM (kg)} = \text{Total Weight (kg)} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Body Fat } \%}{100}\right)`

2. The Katch-McArdle BMR Equation

Developed by Professor Frank Katch and Professor William McArdle, the BMR formula calculates daily calories burned at complete rest based exclusively on lean mass:
`\text{BMR} = 370 + (21.6 \times \text{LBM in kg})`

3. Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) Scaling

Once BMR is established, the script multiplies the baseline figure by your chosen activity coefficient to account for the thermic effect of food, non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT), and structured exercise.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) - Diagnostic Segment

Why is Katch-McArdle often considered superior to Mifflin-St Jeor?

Standard formulas like Mifflin-St Jeor estimate BMR using age, height, and total weight. If two individuals weigh 90 kg, but one has 10% body fat (muscular) and the other has 30% body fat, Mifflin-St Jeor assigns them nearly identical BMRs. Katch-McArdle looks directly at lean mass, recognizing that muscle burns significantly more calories at rest than fat tissue.

How can I accurately measure my body fat percentage?

Accurate body fat measurement ensures precise calculations. Methods include DEXA scans (the gold standard), hydrostatic weighing, skinfold calipers administered by a trained professional, or bioelectrical impedance scales used consistently under identical morning hydration conditions.

How do I adjust my TDEE for fat loss or muscle gain?

Once you find your TDEE maintenance calories, adjust your intake based on your goals. For sustainable fat loss, create a 15% to 20% caloric deficit (typically 300 to 500 kcal below TDEE). For lean muscle gain, add a 10% caloric surplus (200 to 300 kcal above TDEE) combined with progressive resistance training.
